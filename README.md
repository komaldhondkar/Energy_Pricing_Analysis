## Pricing Analysis & Customer Profitability

This project focuses on analyzing the profitability of a cohort of approximately 800 residential household customers in the energy market, with the objective of understanding customer-level profitability, identifying pricing patterns, and generating actionable insights for the pricing team.

The analysis began with a detailed **data preparation and quality assessment** to identify potential data quality issues and determine whether the available data was suitable for profitability analysis. Relevant customer, contract, consumption, billing, acquisition, and procurement cost information was reviewed before performing the profitability calculations.

Customer-level profitability was calculated for each contract using two perspectives: **absolute profit in euros (€)** and **relative profitability in cents per kWh**. This provided a clear view of both the overall financial contribution of individual customers and the profitability generated relative to their electricity consumption.

Key **cohort-level KPIs and summary metrics** were then developed to provide an overall understanding of customer profitability. The analysis also examined how profitability changed across different **contract start months**, helping identify trends and variations over time. Potential explanations for these patterns were considered based on customer behavior, contract characteristics, procurement costs, consumption levels, and energy-market conditions.

The project further analyzed customer groups to identify patterns among the **most and least profitable customers**. Factors such as contract duration, acquisition channel, consumption, billing, and procurement costs were considered to understand differences in profitability between customer segments.

The final analysis was designed to support the pricing team by transforming raw customer and contract data into structured profitability metrics and business insights. Rather than focusing only on numerical results, the project emphasized **critical thinking, data quality, trend analysis, customer segmentation, and hypothesis development** to explain the underlying drivers of pricing and profitability.

### Key Areas

* Customer & contract profitability analysis
* Data quality assessment and preparation
* Profit calculation (€ and cents/kWh)
* Cohort KPI analysis
* Contract start-month profitability trends
* Customer segmentation and pattern analysis
* Acquisition channel analysis
* Consumption and procurement cost analysis
* Pricing and energy-market insights
* Data-driven hypothesis development
